Beyond the swaps
Clean home tips.

Free habits, not purchases — worth knowing regardless of what's on the swap list.

Shoes off at the door
Shoes track in pesticide residue, road grime, and outdoor pollutants that then sit in carpet and dust. A simple no-shoes habit measurably reduces what accumulates on floors your baby will eventually crawl on.
Dust and vacuum with a HEPA filter
Household dust concentrates whatever has been shed from furniture, textiles, and products over time. A HEPA-filtered vacuum captures meaningfully more of it than a standard one — worth checking what your current vacuum uses.
EMF awareness, especially near sleep
Keep phones off the nightstand or in airplane mode overnight, and avoid placing the router in or right beside a bedroom. This matters more once the baby's crib is set up nearby — the same logic behind recommending a low-EMF monitor.
Lighting and circadian rhythm
Blue light suppresses melatonin more strongly in children than adults. Amber or red light in the evening — especially in the nursery — supports better sleep for everyone in the house, not just the baby.
Wivety Amber Sleep Light Bulbs (~$15–20 for a 2-pack, Amazon) screw into any existing lamp — true 1800K amber, no WiFi or Bluetooth required.
New furniture off-gassing
New furniture — especially a crib, mattress, or anything with foam or particleboard — off-gasses VOCs most heavily in the first few weeks. Airing out a room with the windows open for a few hours after new furniture arrives, before baby spends real time in it, meaningfully reduces this.
Humidity monitoring
A basic hygrometer (under $15) helps you keep an eye on indoor humidity. Consistently above 50–60% is worth paying attention to for general air quality — this isn't about detecting anything specific, just general awareness.
Two often-overlooked ones
Radon testing — the EPA recommends every home test for radon regardless of age or location; kits run under $15. Dryer vent cleaning once a year also matters for both fire safety and indoor air quality, since lint buildup restricts airflow back into the house.
