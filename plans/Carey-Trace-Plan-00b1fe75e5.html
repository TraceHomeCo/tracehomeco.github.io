Products you already have
How Trace rates what's in your home.

You have plenty of products around the house, including Mustela from your PR days. We rate every product the same way, so here is how it works.

We read the label for the exact product, not the brand's reputation. One brand can make a clean product and one that isn't. Mustela is a good example: the Stelatopia cleansing oil has a short, fully listed formula with no fragrance, while the Gentle Cleansing Gel lists “Parfum (Fragrance)” and PEG ingredients.

A certification covers only the products named on it. Mustela's own EWG Verified list covers 8 products, so a Mustela label is not an automatic pass.

Four things we check on any label:

  • Fragrance: an undisclosed “fragrance” or “parfum” fails, and essential oils have to be listed one by one.
  • Plastics: nothing plastic in the food, drink or skin path, including polyester, nylon and polymer ingredients.
  • A full ingredient or material list, with nothing hidden.
  • Independent certification or testing, not the brand's own word.

What to do with what you have: use it up, don't throw it out, and swap when you run out. Send us a photo of any label and we'll rate it the same way.

Sleep basics for the bedrooms

Good sleep is shaped as much by the room as by the bed. These habits cost nothing and apply to every bedroom in the house, the boys' rooms most of all.

  • Dark: as dark as you can get it. Blackout curtains help, and any light that stays on should be dim, warm or amber.
  • Light after sunset: bright, blue-rich light in the evening can delay melatonin, so switch to lamps and warm bulbs after dark.
  • Cool and quiet: a slightly cool room and steady, low noise suit most sleepers.
  • Same schedule: a regular bedtime and wake time, including weekends, is one of the strongest sleep habits.
  • Screens and devices out: keep them out of the bedroom, or off or in airplane mode overnight.
  • Clean air: fresh air by day and an air purifier help the room feel better to sleep in.

Educational, not medical advice. If a child's sleep is a concern, raise it with your pediatrician.

Free habits for every room
These cost nothing and help as much as many swaps.
Shoes off
Leave shoes at the door. They track in pesticide residue, dust and pollen that end up on floors where the kids play.
Open windows
Open windows for 10 to 15 minutes a day when the outdoor air is good, so stale indoor air can move out. Check the local air quality first.
Humidity
Keep indoor humidity roughly between 30 and 50%. A small humidity monitor is inexpensive (our link). High humidity supports mold, and very low humidity dries the air.
Lights after dark
Switch off the overhead lights once the sun goes down and use lamps with warm or amber bulbs instead.
Bedroom devices
Keep phones, tablets and laptops out of the bedrooms, or off or in airplane mode overnight. If a device has to stay in the room, put it in airplane mode with Bluetooth off. Charge them across the room or outside the bedroom.
Wireless and appliances
If a smart meter, router or large appliance shares a wall with a bedroom, move the bed or the device when you can. The health evidence on everyday wireless exposure is still debated, so this is a low-cost precaution.

The Trace Scanning Key
What we scan for, and why.

Think of daily exposures like a phone running too many apps at once. No single one crashes it — together, they drain the battery and slow everything down. The goal isn't eliminating every app; it's reducing the background load.

Air & Inhalation
VOCs. Evaporate into the room. If it has a "smell," you're breathing the chemical.
Fragrance. A legal loophole hiding undisclosed ingredients.
Skin Contact
Synthetics. Polyester, nylon, spandex — plastics worn against skin for hours.
Parabens. Preservatives that absorb fast and mimic hormones.
Kitchen, Cookware & Ingestion
PFAS. "Forever chemicals" in non-stick coatings. Never break down.
BPA. In plastics and can linings. Leaches under heat.
Phthalates. Often hidden inside "fragrance." Disrupt hormones.
Water & Heavy Metals
Chlorine / Chloramine. Absorbed through skin, inhaled as steam in hot showers.
Lead. In older plumbing. No safe exposure level exists.
Pharmaceuticals. Trace medication residues that pass through municipal treatment, found in tap water nationally.
Fluoride. A real, contested topic — real dental benefits, but a real court finding on IQ risk too. See the water section below for how this applies to your specific setup.
