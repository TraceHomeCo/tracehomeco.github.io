Bedroom
Air purifierHigh impact
Currently: no air purifier
A third of your life happens in this room. Bedrooms run measurably higher in VOCs than other rooms, since 7–8 hours of closed-door, low-ventilation occupancy overnight lets things accumulate.
A common question worth answering directly: does using a purifier make your body more reactive when you don't have one? No — there's no established mechanism where reducing exposure to actual pollutants makes you more vulnerable later. That's different from things like allergen exposure, which has a more complex relationship with immune function. Cleaner air during the hours you have the most control over (sleep) is just a straightforward reduction in cumulative burden, not a trade-off.

EMFs
EMF awarenessWorth knowing
Simple, free habits matter more than any product here: keep phones off the nightstand or in airplane mode overnight, avoid placing the router in or beside a bedroom, and avoid laptops directly on your lap for extended periods.
Worth flagging honestly: "WiFi protector" stickers or plug-in devices that claim to neutralize router EMF without reducing the actual signal don't have credible independent testing behind them — a router has to emit signal to function, so nothing can "protect" against that without physically blocking or moving it. A Faraday bag is different and genuinely effective, since it fully encloses a device (like a phone overnight) in a signal-blocking material — real physics, not a claim.

Beyond the swaps
Clean home tips.

Free habits, not purchases — worth knowing regardless of what's on the swap list.

With grandkids visiting
Since grandkids play at the house regularly, it's worth treating floor-level products (rugs, cleaning sprays, dust) with a bit more attention — kids spend far more time at floor level than adults do.
Dust and vacuum with a HEPA filter
Household dust concentrates whatever has been shed from furniture, textiles, and products over time. A HEPA-filtered vacuum captures meaningfully more of it than a standard one.
New furniture off-gassing
New furniture off-gasses most heavily in the first few weeks. Airing out a room with windows open for a few hours after new furniture arrives meaningfully reduces this.
Humidity monitoring
A basic hygrometer (under $15) helps you keep an eye on indoor humidity. Consistently above 50–60% is worth paying attention to for general air quality.
Going deeper
There's always a next layer.

This plan covers what came up in the visit and in conversation — but a home is never fully "done." No need to think about any of this now. Just good to know it's there whenever the timing makes sense.

Furniture & upholstery
Couches, chairs, and anything with foam padding can off-gas for years, and many are treated with flame retardants. A slower, bigger category since most non-toxic options are also the most expensive — worth tackling piece by piece rather than all at once.
Textiles beyond sheets
Curtains, rugs, and towels all carry the same fabric-treatment questions sheets do — synthetic dyes, wrinkle-resistant finishes, flame retardants on rugs specifically. Worth a look once the higher-exposure categories in this plan are handled.
Whole-home HVAC filtration
A portable air purifier treats one room. A MERV-13 filter in your central HVAC system treats every room the system reaches, continuously — a bigger, one-time upgrade that complements rather than replaces the portable units already in this plan.
Plumbing & water heater materials
Worth knowing for a home renovation down the line: PEX plumbing (common in newer construction) is generally considered safe, but older galvanized or lead-soldered pipes are a genuine concern in older homes. Not something to investigate today — just worth asking about if any plumbing work comes up.
