The Trace Scanning Key
What we scan for, and why.

Think of daily exposures like apps running in the background of a phone. No single one crashes it; together they add load. The goal isn't eliminating every one, it's reducing the background load where it's easiest to do.

Air & inhalation
VOCs. Compounds that evaporate into room air from paints, cleaners and furnishings. If it has a strong smell, you are breathing part of it.
Fragrance. A legal category that lets brands list one word in place of many undisclosed ingredients.
Skin contact
Synthetics. Polyester, nylon and spandex are plastics worn against skin for hours.
Parabens. Preservatives that absorb through skin and are studied for hormone-like activity.
Kitchen, cookware & ingestion
PFAS. "Forever chemicals" used in non-stick and stain-resistant coatings. They do not break down.
BPA & related plastics. Found in some plastics and can linings; can migrate into food, especially with heat.
Phthalates. Often part of an undisclosed "fragrance" blend; studied for hormone disruption.
Microplastics. Tiny plastic fragments from plastic containers, bottles and synthetic fabrics. They end up in food, water and dust.
Water & metals
Chlorine / chloramine. Disinfectants in municipal water; absorbed through skin and inhaled as steam in hot showers.
Lead. Can come from older plumbing. No safe exposure level has been identified.
Fluoride. A topic you will know well. We do not take a side: we note what a product does or does not remove and leave the choice to you.
Habits, not hazards
Light & devices. Evening light colour and nighttime WiFi/phone use are habits that affect sleep. We flag them as easy adjustments, not contaminants.
The gap we're navigating: a product on a shelf isn't proven safe. It usually means no one has been required to prove otherwise. This plan is built on ingredient disclosure and independent testing or certification wherever it exists, not on marketing claims.

Whole-home system (Flowtech) + Mountain Valley glass deliveryConfirmed good
Drinking from glass-bottled spring water avoids plastic contact entirely, and in the Trace standard it sits at the same top tier as a certified filter or reverse osmosis. Your Flowtech system treats the water at every tap. Because you mentioned possibly adding reverse osmosis under the sink, that would only be an option if you want a second source for drinking.
Worth knowing: a water softener reduces hardness (scale) but does not remove chlorine, chloramine or most contaminants. San Diego's tap water is disinfected with chloramine, which many carbon systems also do not remove reliably. The whole-home filters in "When You're Ready" add that layer, and an RO at the kitchen sink is the next step for cooking and coffee. On fluoride, we leave that choice with you; spring waters vary, so the brand's published analysis lists the level.
