A Practical Guide to Lower-Tox Living During Your Fertility Journey

Co-authored by Morgan Price Wellness and Trace, for the (In)Fertility Circle

“Fertility treatment already comes with so much to think about. Lower-tox living doesn’t need to become another source of stress. You don’t need to change everything at once. Start with what feels manageable, focus on the things you use most often, and make more intentional swaps as things run out or need replacing.

As part of the (In)Fertility Circle, I wanted to offer practical guidance for women who are interested in reducing everyday exposures while navigating fertility treatment, without adding more pressure to an already overwhelming season. I partnered with Trace to combine that supportive, realistic approach with their expertise in helping you better understand your home and make informed, lower-tox swaps.”

A note from Morgan

Small steps, real research, no pressure

This is educational information, not medical advice. If you’re navigating fertility challenges, please continue working with your care team. This guide is meant to complement that relationship and support you through this journey, not replace it.

Air + water

Water and indoor air are part of our everyday environment, making them practical places to start.

  • Check your local water quality (a free lookup takes minutes)
  • Consider drinking water filtration based on your local water quality
  • Consider shower filtration if chlorine is a concern in your local water
  • Open windows regularly; reduce fragrance sources like candles and plug-ins
  • Consider an air purifier, especially in the bedroom

Kitchen

Heat and plastic are a common, easy-to-avoid combination.

  • Avoid heating food in plastic
  • Choose glass for food storage
  • Consider wood or stainless for cutting boards and everyday utensils
  • Replace scratched or worn non-stick with cast iron or stainless when it’s time
Options to consider
Cookware Lodge (cast iron) Made In (stainless)

Cleaning + laundry

Reducing fragrance is one simple way to make more intentional choices at home.

  • Choose fragrance-free cleaning products
  • Look for simpler products with clearly listed ingredients
  • Choose fragrance-free laundry detergent
Options to consider
Cleaning Branch Basics Molly’s Suds Force of Nature

Personal care + everyday wear

Prioritize what you use most often and what comes into direct contact with your body.

  • Start with the personal care products you use most often
  • Explore alternatives for deodorant, lotion, skincare, and makeup
  • As clothing needs replacing, consider choosing natural fibers like cotton, linen, and wool over synthetic fabrics like polyester
